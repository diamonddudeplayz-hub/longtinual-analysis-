## 1. The through-line

**You do not trade on facts other people lack. You trade on the lag between a fact becoming public and the measurement convention that would price it becoming available — and in all eighteen posts you locate the error in the convention, never in the information.**

You say this yourself, once, in post 12 (Jun 24): *"The gap is not information asymmetry. It is methodology."* That sentence is the whole corpus.

The unit, not the number, is the subject every time:

- **ITRI (P8):** *"The same 18× Osborne applies to Outcomes, produces $130 on EBITDA and $326 on ARR. The difference is entirely the choice of denominator on the software layer."*
- **ADBE (P2):** *"The multiple reflects a seat-count framework, while the reported numbers reflect a consumption framework. Both are looking at the same business."*
- **LNG (P3):** *"Consensus treats geopolitical resolution and supply restoration as the same event. They are not."* — one variable doing two jobs.
- **Gold (P11):** the real-yields regression is the wrong instrument because the marginal buyer changed in 2022. *"Central banks do not trade gold against US real yields."*
- **WPM (P11):** *"the same 28x multiple applied to a bigger number"* — the ITRI denominator move, repointed at a streamer.
- **HK internet (P17):** matched growth and margin to isolate jurisdiction as the only remaining variable.
- **NVDA (P18):** *"the residual values are rent"* — the chart is mislabeled, not wrong.

And the second half — why a public fact stays unpriced — is equally consistent:

- P1: *"Models are adjusted incrementally rather than rebuilt, allowing the misclassification to persist."*
- P12: *"A blank cell requires an assumption with no data behind it."*
- P13: *"The methodology is not wrong. It is incomplete. The incompleteness is the opportunity."*

Note what the through-line is **not**. It is not "primary sources beat narrative," which is the publication's own framing and which fails on four posts: 10, 11, 15 and 17 have no company filing at the centre, and 17 is built almost entirely on LSEG/TIKR consensus data and broker surveys. The brand describes the method of the first half of the corpus. The actual invariant is the convention-lag claim, and that one holds across all eighteen.

## 2. How the work changed — four thresholds, plus gradual drift

Mostly threshold, consolidated gradually in between. Four datable shifts.

### T1 — 24 April 2026 (P4, ADBE CFO): the thesis can now fail by being right

*"RPO growth at or above 10% YoY for four consecutive quarters AND Firefly ARR crosses $500M AND NTM EV/EBITDA remains below 10× through end of FY2027 → exit review. The market has had sufficient data to reclassify and refused to."*

Before this, every break condition in the corpus was about the company deteriorating. This is the first one where you lose by being correct and unpaid. It becomes load-bearing in P9 (*"continuing to hold a position that the market has permanently discounted is its own risk"*) and is the intellectual precondition for P16 existing at all. "Pushback welcome" also starts here.

### T2 — 24 June 2026 (P12, NVDA): a mechanism replaces a complaint

P1 (Mar 19) explained the mispricing with motive: *"Analysts are disincentivized from publishing targets materially above consensus."* P12 replaces that with structure: *"The answer is structural, not analytical... Sell-side models for large-cap technology companies are built as top-line extrapolations from guidance... A blank cell requires an assumption with no data behind it."*

This is the most important shift in the corpus. It converts a grievance into a generative theory — one that predicts *which* facts stay unpriced and for how long. It completes in P13 (Aug 3), where you defend the analysts you used to impugn: *"This is not analytical failure. It is a structural constraint on how sell-side models get built."*

### T3 — 8 September 2026 (P15, Gold p2): the integrity threshold

*"The World Gold Council has since revised that number to 57 tonnes. That is not a rounding error. It is a 77% downward revision to the data point I weighted the most... the whole point of pre-registering break conditions is that you check them when the data comes in. Not only when it flatters you."*

This is measurable, not impressionistic. First-person density (I/my/me per 1,000 words) runs 0.4–2.1 across posts 1–14. P15: **11.0**. The voice becomes personal in the exact post where you are wrong — see §6.

### T4 — 14 Sep / 6 Oct (P16, P18): adversarial self-instrumentation

P16 is the first post built on a private dataset (23 quarters of line items) used *against* your own position, and the first to catch management rather than quote them approvingly: *"A seasonal defense that requires the listener not to notice a directional change is not a complete defense."*

P18 extends it to a named opponent and to a formal rule register with set-dates and actions: *"Set Oct 5, 2026. Action: review at $3.93, exit review at $2.85."*

### Gradual, not threshold

- **Length:** 1,125 → 5,499 words, broadly monotonic.
- **"Verbatim" decay:** 4.4/1k (P1), 5.4 (P7), 5.1 (P10) → 0.6 (P13), 0.7 (P14) → **0.0 in P15, P17, P18.** See §6.

## 3. Recurring failure mode and recurring strength

### Strength

**You can find the one measurement convention that reprices everything if changed, and you can usually prove it is a convention rather than a fact.**

The three best instances:
- **P8:** hold every assumption in TD Cowen's model identical, change only the denominator on the software layer. The argument is unanswerable on its own terms because you borrowed the opponent's inputs.
- **P11:** the real-yields regression broke in 2022 *with a mechanism* — the marginal buyer changed from price-sensitive Western capital to price-insensitive central banks. Plus the best analytical sentence in the corpus: *"A safe-haven trade that requires de-escalation rather than escalation to perform well is not behaving like a normal safe-haven trade. It is behaving like a rates and dollar trade that happens to share a chart with geopolitical risk."*
- **P18:** you read Silicon Data's own one-page methodology and establish that the "values" are a DCF of projected rent, which makes the chart mislabeled rather than false — and you prove it with the chip that didn't change: *"Silicon Data's H100 residual value was $14,976 in November 2025 and $22,068 by June 2026, up 47% in seven months on the same piece of silicon. The chip didn't get any better. Rent did."*

### Failure mode

**You write falsifiable conditions with real precision, and when one actually fires you relitigate it instead of acting on it.**

Four instances, three names:

1. **ITRI Investor Day.** P1 (Mar 19) break condition #3: *"Investor Day delayed beyond Q1 2027."* P5 (Apr 29), same item: *"An Investor Day delay beyond Q1 2027 extends the thesis — it does not end it."* Reclassified from thesis-breaker to non-event in five weeks, unannounced, then repeated in P13. The Investor Day has been "the forcing function" in P1, P5, P8 and P13 — four posts, four and a half months — and has never been scheduled.
2. **Gold H1 pace.** P15: *"On a strict read, H1 triggered it. I'm not calling it yet because the Q1 drag is explained by specific country events."* You name the exception as you take it — *"if Q3 doesn't hold near Q2's pace, I won't have that out"* — which is better than concealment. The condition still fired and you still held.
3. **Adobe's unbreakable floor.** P2: *"Absolute Firefly ARR below $250 million in any single reported quarter triggers an immediate exit"* — set at a level Firefly had already cleared while growing 50–75% QoQ. A condition that cannot fire is decoration. Meanwhile RPO, the metric that actually deteriorated (13% → 8%), got absorbed into a *"composite: mixed"* grade in P16 rather than scored on its own terms.
4. **Cheniere.** Position disclosed in P3 and P6, gates pre-registered for the May 7 print — and then twelve subsequent posts across five months with zero follow-up. The one name whose conditions were never scored at all.

### They are the same thing

Your edge is the claim that a convention is wrong. A convention is, by construction, a thing that can stay wrong indefinitely. So the strength requires you to be early against a measurement regime, and the failure mode is that *"the regime hasn't changed yet"* and *"I was wrong"* produce identical evidence — and your own framework supplies a respectable reason to prefer the first reading every single time.

You have already written the diagnosis. P16: *"At a certain point, repeating that the market is wrong stops being an analytical position and starts being a rationalization."* The sentence is in the corpus. Seven weeks later P18 carries nine break conditions, eight inherited, all marked "Not triggered."

### Secondary weakness: errors cluster in the rhetoric, never in the mechanism

- **P2:** *"Adobe at 7.73× ... trades at a 131% discount to ServiceNow."* 7.73 against 17.88 is a 57% discount; ServiceNow carries the 131% premium. Stated backwards.
- **P2:** *"Adobe's 10-year average multiple is 29×."* P4, twenty-three days later: *"10-year mean 22.55×."* Same stock, same metric, no reconciliation.
- **P3:** headline is *"Cheniere Has a $6.6B Floor and a $255 Price Target"* — but $255 is TD Cowen's target, disclosed as such in the body, and the stock was $259.68. The headline sells someone else's number, below spot, as the destination.
- **P17:** the headline *"44 to 46% discount at matched growth rates"* rests on two pairs, both containing NetEase. Your own body reports the pairs that break it (JD/Pinterest 16%; Bilibili at a premium).

Every one of these is in a headline or a punchline. None is in a mechanism section. That's a function of wanting the line to land, not of weak arithmetic.

## 4. Climbing vs plateaued

### Still climbing

- **Adversarial handling of your own thesis.** P9 → P15 → P16 → P18 is a clean monotonic line. P18 reaches a new level: *"That's the real risk to my position, and it's a bet on the multiple rather than the GPU math in his post. Price isn't on my ledger by design."* First time you isolate the risk your framework structurally cannot see and state what you'd do anyway.
- **Capital-structure reasoning.** Absent from posts 1–10 entirely. P11 introduces instrument selection (metal / producer / streamer). P18 asks where the loss lands and in what order: *"NVIDIA is hurt last and in a particular order. First, nothing happens... Then the guarantees and revenue floors start costing it cash. Then, if the trouble lasts, the order book takes the hit."* Fastest-improving dimension in the corpus.
- **Opponent quality.** "The market" (abstraction, P1–P9) → the rotation trade (P10) → sell-side model architecture (P12–13) → your own prior number (P15) → management's seasonal defence (P16) → a named investor with a dated, strike-priced, expiring position (P18). Each harder than the last.
- **Witness-incentive theory.** P18 contains the best methodological paragraph in the corpus: *"The memory makers say that doesn't happen before 2028. They would say that, since they're the ones selling into the shortage, so I wanted evidence from people who'd rather it weren't true."* Then you rank — the buyer cutting its own margin guidance, $279B of purchase commitments, fab construction lead times (*"which nobody can talk up"*), and only last the sellers' forecasts. Nothing in posts 1–14 does this; early posts treat all management verbatim as equally probative.

### Plateaued

- **Prose devices.** The isolated-word paragraph has run at one setting since April: *"Accelerant. Not offset."* (P4), *"And. Not instead of."* (P4), *"Astounded."* (P6), *"The unanimity is the point."* (P13).
- **Position sizing and portfolio construction.** Nonexistent across all eighteen. No weight, no cost basis, no sizing change, in a corpus whose recurring question is literally hold-or-exit.
- **The catalyst-as-forcing-function move.** ITRI's Investor Day (P1/P5/P8/P13) and Adobe's "next gate" (P2/P4/P7/P9) use an identical structure for six months with no update to the prior probability that the gate fires on time.
- **Break-condition *authoring*,** as distinct from reporting. P18's rules 6–9 are the best-specified in the corpus. Rules 1–5 carry *"forward from my August 28 post, word for word"* — including rule 3, which needs a hyperscaler to say a specific thing on two consecutive calls **and** a sequential Hyperscale decline. A conjunction that narrow is the same defect as Adobe's $250M Firefly floor from April.

### Untested — what would change my read

1. **An exit.** Eighteen posts, six names, zero closed positions, zero realized losses. Every thesis is live. Nothing in the corpus demonstrates that your break conditions can produce a sale, and you have twice declined to act on one that fired. P16's *"Post 5 will either be a position increase or an exit"* is the only binary pre-commitment and it hasn't come due.
2. **A name where the convention turns out to be correct.** Every subject is one where you think the measurement regime is wrong. You have never published a pass. P17 comes closest and then pitches Bilibili anyway, after conceding it *"already trades above where a US company at its current margins would trade."* A "framework ran, discount was justified, I passed" post would tell me whether the framework discriminates or only confirms.
3. **A thesis that loses a leg and doesn't gain one.** Note P15's structure: the leg you anchored on was revised down 77%, and in the same post a new supporting leg (fiscal dominance) appeared — the one leg with no break condition attached. You never flag that pattern. Thesis-preserving elaboration is the risk and it's currently unobserved.
4. **Cheniere.** The unscored position. Grading the May 7 gates now — win, loss or unclear — is the single most informative thing you could publish about your own reliability, precisely because it's the one you dropped.

## 6. What you probably haven't noticed

**You have stopped saying "verbatim," and it hit zero exactly as your reasoning started carrying the argument.**

Occurrences per 1,000 words: 4.4 (P1), 2.1 (P2), 2.5 (P3), 3.8 (P4), 5.4 (P7), 4.2 (P9), 5.1 (P10) — then 0.6 (P13), 0.7 (P14), and **0.0 in P15, P17 and P18.**

The early posts needed to certify that you'd read the transcript, because the reading *was* the edge: a quote with speaker, role and date attached was the product. The late posts don't need it, because by P18 the edge is the *ordering of witnesses by incentive* — *"I wanted evidence from people who'd rather it weren't true"* — and a certification adds nothing to that. You outgrew your own brand mechanic without, as far as the corpus shows, noticing. The masthead still says Primary Source Arbitrage, but your two best posts win on inference *from* sources, not possession *of* them. P17 is the control case: almost no primary source, and it's the weakest post of the late period.

Two smaller ones, visible only across the whole set:

**You write in the first person when you have something to lose and in the third person when you're adjudicating.** First-person density goes 0.4 per 1,000 words in P14 → 11.0 in P15. The voice became personal in precisely the post where you were wrong, and stayed personal in the post where you take on a named opponent (P18, 8.2). Posts 1–14 are a disembodied analytical voice; P15 onward is a person with a record. That's a tell about where you think authority comes from.

**Six of eighteen posts turn on absence as evidence, and you've never named it as a technique.** No analyst asked about organic ARR (P5); Resiliency Solutions doesn't appear in the SOTP (P8); the blank cell (P12); no desk publishes an EV/ARR SOTP (P13); the incoming CEO *"was asked to identify the biggest competitive threat and chose not to"* (P16); NVIDIA quietly shopping residual risk to insurers (P18). You're unusually good at reading what isn't said or modelled. Because it's unnamed, you deploy it by instinct rather than on purpose.

## 7. The next capability, and what the post looks like

Every dimension in §4 is converging on one thing nothing in the corpus has done: **apply the framework to a position you own and conclude against yourself, with the sale.**

Why this and not something else: the arc P9 (concede a bear point) → P15 (concede a number) → P16 (build a dataset that cuts against you) → P18 (concede the opponent's central claim) is a monotonic increase in how much you'll concede *while still holding*. There is exactly one step left on that line. And the corpus has an unscored position (Cheniere), two conditions that fired without action (ITRI's Investor Day, gold's H1 pace), and a pre-commitment coming due (P16). The next capability isn't better writing. It's demonstrating the apparatus has teeth.

**The post:**

- **Title names a decision, not a question or a surprising fact.** All eighteen current titles are one of those two. Something nearer *"I Was Wrong About the Forcing Function. I Sold Itron."*
- **Opens with the condition that fired and the date, not with the thesis.** For ITRI: break condition #3 as published on March 19, next to the sentence you published on April 29 — *"extends the thesis — it does not end it"* — with the admission that you changed the rule rather than the position. That paragraph is the one only you can write and nobody else can check you on.
- **Separates "was the analysis wrong" from "was the trade wrong."** The Itron analysis was largely right: margins beat the 2027 model two years early, ARR compounded, the stock did eventually move 26% in a day. What was wrong was the catalyst's clock and your willingness to re-date it four times. Different errors. Your framework is strong on the first and has no machinery at all for the second.
- **Prices the opportunity cost.** You gestured once — *"the opportunity cost of holding a position where the forcing function keeps receding is real"* (P13) — and never computed it. Compute it: capital in ITRI from March 19 at $91.79, against the alternatives you were writing up yourself in the same months.
- **Either keeps the thesis alive without the position, or kills it.** Your framework honestly allows "the convention is still wrong and I no longer want to be the one waiting." That's exactly the conclusion P15 reached about data precision — *"the precision of any individual quarter matters less than the multi-year trend"* — without ever reaching it about your own holding period.
- **Ends with the first time-boxed break condition in eighteen posts.** Every condition in the corpus is a level: ARR <15%, margin <72%, rent <$2.85. **None is a date.** That's the gap, and it is the gap that produced the failure mode in §3. The rule that would have caught ITRI in April and gold in September is: *if the forcing function has not been scheduled by [date], the position is cut regardless of the fundamentals.*

**Cheaper version, if you want to test the capability before committing to it:** the Cheniere post. Five months late, two pre-registered gates unscored, position disclosed, thread silently dropped. Scoring it costs one post and buys the one thing the corpus cannot currently demonstrate.

- I could not verify any external data — Silicon Data indices, TIKR and LSEG multiples, consensus figures, WGC revisions. This assesses reasoning structure, internal consistency and sourcing discipline, not whether the underlying numbers are right.
- I don't have your positions, sizing, cost bases, reader comments, or any paywalled sections. "Zero exits" means zero exits *visible in the corpus*; if you've sold things without writing them up, that changes §4's first untested item but strengthens the point about the published record.
